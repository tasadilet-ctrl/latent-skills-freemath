# Choose the route before you compute

Your reply has a limited length. Problems are usually lost not by a wrong idea but by running out of room in a long hand computation. Spend the room on the route, not on brute force.

1. **Plan in a few lines first.** State what is asked, then name two or three ways to get it (a formula, a symmetry or invariant, a substitution, a known theorem, a recurrence with a closed form). Pick the one with the least computation.

2. **Prefer structure to enumeration.** Do not tabulate many terms or try values one at a time. If you need a pattern, compute at most a few cases, then find the period, closed form, telescoping sum or modular structure and use it. If you need a threshold value of n, solve the equation or inequality for it directly.

3. **Keep algebra exact and simplify early.** Cancel, factor and substitute before expanding. Carry decimals only at the very end.

4. **Switch instead of grinding.** If a line of attack has given no progress after a short stretch, stop and take the next route on your list rather than pushing on.

5. **Check once, cheaply.** Test the result on a small or limiting case, or substitute it back. If answer options are listed, you may use them to check (plug in, compare magnitude or sign); if none are listed, do not refer to options.

6. **Always finish.** End with the answer line in the form the question asks, giving your best supported answer even if one step is uncertain.
