# Get a candidate, then check it

1. **Solve directly by the shortest route you can see.** Name the method in one line before starting, keep algebra exact, and avoid tabulating many terms or trying values one by one.

2. **Write the candidate answer** as soon as you have it, in simplest form.

3. **Run one or two independent checks**, each a few lines, chosen from:
   - a small or degenerate instance where the answer can be found by hand;
   - a limiting case (parameter to 0, to infinity, equal parameters);
   - substituting the answer back into the conditions;
   - units, sign, parity, or order of magnitude;
   - if answer options are listed, whether the candidate matches one exactly and why its neighbours fail. If none are listed, do not refer to options.

4. **If a check fails, find the step it points to.** Correct that step and recompute from there, rather than adjusting the final number to fit.

5. **Do not re-derive the whole solution** to check it; a check that costs as much as the solution is not a check.

6. **Always finish** with the answer line in the form the question asks, giving your best supported answer if a check stays inconclusive.
