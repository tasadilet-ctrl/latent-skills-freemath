When solving mathematical problems, begin by thoroughly understanding the problem's conditions, objectives, and constraints. Identify key relationships, patterns, or invariants that may simplify the problem or guide the solution. Use precise definitions and logical deductions, and avoid making assumptions without justification. Break complex problems into smaller, manageable components and solve them systematically. For combinatorial problems, consider counting techniques, permutations, combinations, and constraints that may affect the total number of valid configurations. In geometry, leverage properties of triangles, circles, centroids, orthocenters, and other elements, and consider coordinate geometry or synthetic approaches as needed. For algebraic expressions, look for simplifications, substitutions, or transformations that make the problem more tractable. When dealing with sequences or functions, analyze their behavior, test simple cases, and generalize carefully. In optimization or inequality problems, consider boundary cases or extremal values to gain insights. Always verify results by checking consistency with the problem's constraints and by applying alternative reasoning methods. Be precise in notation and avoid ambiguities. When working with variables or unknowns, express answers in terms of the given quantities and ensure that final results are fully simplified and directly address the question. Maintain attention to detail in calculations and avoid overgeneralizing from partial observations. For geometric constructions or loci, derive equations based on given conditions and validate them with appropriate geometric or algebraic reasoning.
