Solve problems by breaking them into logical steps, ensuring each step is justified and follows from the previous one. Start by clearly identifying what is given and what is to be found. Use definitions and theorems appropriately, and avoid assuming information not explicitly stated. When dealing with geometric configurations, draw diagrams to visualize relationships and apply coordinate geometry or synthetic methods as needed. For algebraic problems, manipulate equations systematically, and check for consistency. When encountering unknowns or complex expressions, consider introducing variables or using symmetry to simplify the problem. Always verify intermediate results and consider edge cases to ensure generality. When working with inequalities or optimization, analyze the behavior of functions and consider extremal cases. Maintain clarity in notation and ensure that all steps are explained in a way that could be followed by someone with a basic understanding of the subject. Finally, double-check calculations and ensure that the final answer is expressed in the required form.
