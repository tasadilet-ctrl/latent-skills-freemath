To accurately perform matrix transformations, follow this systematic procedure:

1. **Understand the input matrix and all operations**. Begin by confirming the matrix's dimensions and the precise definition of each transformation, including direction (clockwise/counterclockwise), axis (horizontal/vertical), or other specifics.

2. **Apply operations in the exact given order**, without altering, skipping, or reinterpreting any step. Maintain strict sequence adherence throughout the process.

3. **Clarify the meaning of each transformation** (e.g., mirroring along a diagonal, rotating, transposing, cropping, or mapping values) to prevent conceptual errors. Use definitions that align with standard matrix transformation conventions.

4. **Verify the matrix after each transformation**. Confirm the dimensions and values to ensure correctness and detect errors early.

5. **Use consistent indexing** (e.g., 1-indexed or 0-indexed) and apply transformations precisely, particularly when dealing with row/column removal, cropping, or mirroring.

6. **Be mindful of edge cases**, such as singular rows or columns, transformations that leave the matrix unchanged, or operations that affect only specific elements or values.

7. **Review the full sequence of operations** before finalizing the result. Ensure that no steps were missed, misapplied, or misinterpreted, and that all arithmetic or logical steps were executed correctly.

8. **Present the final matrix** in the required format, ensuring that the values, alignment, and formatting are correct, with no extraneous text or symbols.

By adhering to these structured steps, the model can ensure precision, consistency, and correctness in executing matrix transformations.
