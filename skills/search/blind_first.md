# Predict the Result, Then Test the Options

1. **Before reading the options, predict.** From the setup alone, write
   two or three lines: what quantity is controlled, in what regime, in
   what form (equality, two-sided bound, one-sided bound, classification),
   and what the constants may depend on. This is a hypothesis, not a fact.

2. **Restate the definitions exactly.** Copy signs, normalisations and the
   precise objects named in the question; check your prediction uses them
   correctly.

3. **Test each option against the hypotheses, not against your
   prediction.** An option is false if it needs something the setup does
   not supply: an extra converse or characterisation, a uniformity or
   independence that nothing gives, a closed endpoint, a global statement
   from local data. Your prediction only tells you where to look.

4. **Revise when an option is better supported than your guess.** If an
   option is consistent with every hypothesis and says more than your
   prediction, your prediction was probably too weak.

5. **Pick the strongest option that survives step 3.** Keep the reply
   brief, then give the answer line.
