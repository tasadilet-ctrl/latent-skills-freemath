# Audit Every Clause Against the Hypotheses

1. **Fix the definitions exactly as written.** Restate the key objects in one
   or two lines, copying signs, normalisations and which operator or space a
   symbol refers to. Most wrong answers start from a misread definition.

2. **Name the mechanism.** In one line, say what the hypotheses make
   available (a spectral gap, compactness, a monotonicity formula, a counting
   argument ...) and what kind of conclusion that mechanism normally yields.

3. **Split each option into its clauses.** Every clause is a separate claim
   that needs its own justification: an added converse, an "if and only if",
   "every" instead of "some", "uniformly" or "independent of", a closed
   endpoint, a global instead of a local statement. An option is false if any
   one of its clauses fails.

4. **Distrust your own quick calculations.** If a back-of-the-envelope
   computation rules an option out, check its assumptions once (independence,
   signs, scaling, the definition in step 1) before relying on it.

5. **Choose.** Among options whose clauses all survive, pick the one that
   says the most. An option implied by another surviving option is not the
   answer.

6. **Be brief.** A few lines per option. End with the answer line.
