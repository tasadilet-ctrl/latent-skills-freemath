# House Style

1. **Write in continuous prose.** Sentences carry the material; reserve lists
   for things that are genuinely enumerable in the source, and do not convert
   a paragraph of argument into bullets for its own sake.

2. **Keep the tense consistent.** Mathematical statements are conventionally
   present tense; stay in it rather than drifting into the past when narrating
   a construction or into the future when describing a consequence.

3. **Prefer the active voice** where an agent exists, and the passive only
   where the agent is genuinely irrelevant or unknown, rather than alternating
   between them within a single line of argument.

4. **Introduce an object before using it.** A symbol should be named at its
   first appearance rather than several clauses later, and a term of art should
   appear in the form the field uses.

5. **Punctuate displayed material as part of the sentence** that contains it,
   so a display ending a sentence takes its terminal punctuation and one in
   apposition takes a comma.

6. **Keep parenthetical remarks short.** Where an aside runs longer than the
   clause containing it, give it its own sentence rather than nesting it.

7. **Use one name per object throughout.** Once something has been called a
   bound, do not later call it an estimate or an inequality as if it were a
   different thing.
