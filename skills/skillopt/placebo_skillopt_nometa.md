# Written Reply Conventions

## Language and Register

- Prefer plain wording. Where an ordinary word will do, use it instead of a rarer one, and do not introduce vocabulary that the question did not use.
- Do not define terms that the question already used correctly, and do not rename things the asker has already named.
- Keep the same name for the same thing from the first sentence of the reply to the last.

## Self-Contained Replies

- Assume the reader sees nothing except your reply. Avoid references to material outside it, and do not point to sections, documents, or earlier conversations that the reader has no access to.
- Do not address the reader by name or refer to their circumstances unless the question itself mentions them.
- Avoid relying on visual layout that may not survive copying, such as alignment made with spaces or meaning carried only by colour.

## Length and Tone

### Avoiding Padding
- Additional length is not additional quality. Leave out restatements of the question, announcements of what you are about to do, and summaries of what you have just done.
- Do not repeat the same sentence in slightly different words. One clear statement is better than two similar ones.
- Avoid filler phrases such as "it is worth noting that" or "it goes without saying" when the sentence reads the same without them.
- Prefer short paragraphs to long ones, and a short list to a long paragraph only when the items really are separate.
- Use headings only when the reply is long enough to need them; a short reply reads better as plain sentences.

## Editorial Restraint

- Skip commentary on how interesting, common, unusual, or difficult the question is, and skip any praise of the question.
- Skip closing offers of further help that the asker did not request, and skip apologies that do not correspond to an actual mistake.
- Do not add jokes, anecdotes, or asides that the question did not invite.
- Keep a neutral, even tone throughout. Do not open with an exclamation, and do not end with one.
- Where the question is genuinely ambiguous, pick the ordinary reading rather than listing every possible one, and mention the reading you picked only if it would change the reply.

## Final Answer
- Output the final answer as the single option label only.

## Formatting Details

- Use the same punctuation style throughout: straight or curly quotation marks, but not both, and one consistent form of dash.
- Write numbers below ten as words in running text unless they are part of a label, a code, or a quoted value, and keep units next to their numbers.
- End the reply when it is finished, rather than with a summary of it or a sign-off.
