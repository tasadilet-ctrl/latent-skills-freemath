# Derive First, Then Take the Strongest That Survives

1. **State what must be proved.** One line naming the object, the property, the
   limit in question, and the hypotheses granted. Every candidate is judged
   against that line and nothing else.

2. **Drop candidates about something else.** A candidate concerning another
   object, another limit, or a conclusion the setup never raises is wrong
   however true it sounds. Remove these first: it is the cheapest elimination
   available and it removes the most candidates.

3. **Ask of each survivor whether the setup actually gives it.** Try to derive
   it from the hypotheses. Name the step that yields it, or name the step it
   needs and does not have. A candidate you cannot derive is false, whatever
   its strength and however familiar it looks.

4. **Check the three places derivations fail.** A mode of convergence upgraded
   past what is available; a bound claimed on a fixed region when only a
   shrinking one is controlled; a uniformity nothing supplies. Test every
   survivor against all three by name rather than in passing.

5. **Among the candidates that do follow, take the strongest.** A derivable
   candidate that another derivable candidate implies is the classic wrong
   answer: true, and not what was asked. Compare the survivors pairwise and say
   which implies which. Strength decides only here, never in step 3.

6. **If two still remain, name the case that separates them** and decide which
   the hypotheses support there, rather than which sounds more substantial.

7. **Give the letter alone** on the final line.
