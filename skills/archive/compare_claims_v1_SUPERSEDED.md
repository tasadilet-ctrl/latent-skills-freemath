# Identify the Claim, Compare the Candidates, Eliminate

1. **Name what is being asked for.** Before reading any candidate, write one
   line stating which claim the question wants: the strongest provable
   statement, the exact value, the correct characterisation. A question asking
   for the strongest statement is not answered by any true statement.

2. **Fix the hypotheses.** List the assumptions actually granted, quantifiers
   intact. Separate "for every" from "for all sufficiently small", "exists"
   from "exists uniquely", and pointwise from uniform. Wrong candidates usually
   differ from the right one in exactly one of these.

3. **Restate each candidate plainly.** Write what each asserts in your own
   words, naming the object quantified over and the sense of any convergence,
   bound, or equality. Do not judge yet; a candidate that resists restatement
   is one you have not finished reading.

4. **Order the candidates by strength.** Work out which imply which. A
   statement that follows from another is weaker, and a weaker true statement
   is the standard wrong answer. Note any two that are equivalent as stated.

5. **Test each against the hypotheses.** Check for over-claiming — a dropped
   assumption, an upgraded mode of convergence, a bound strengthened past what
   the assumptions support — and for under-claiming, where the candidate holds
   but says less than the hypotheses already give.

6. **Eliminate by construction, not by plausibility.** Reject a candidate only
   when you can name the degenerate case, boundary value, or counterexample
   that breaks it, or the step whose assumption it violates.

7. **Choose the strongest survivor** and give its letter alone on the final
   line, with no restatement of the reasoning.
