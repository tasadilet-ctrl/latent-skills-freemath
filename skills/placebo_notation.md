# Presentation Conventions

1. **Keep the notation the question uses.** Where a symbol, variable name, or
   function is introduced in the statement, carry it through unchanged rather
   than renaming it or substituting a more familiar equivalent.

2. **Write set and index names as given.** If the statement indexes over one
   letter, keep that letter; do not silently shift between equivalent indices
   or reorder the arguments of a pairing already fixed by the question.

3. **Match the typographic form of expressions.** An expression displayed on
   its own line in the statement stays displayed; one written inline stays
   inline. Do not convert between fraction forms that the question has fixed.

4. **Preserve the spelling conventions of the statement.** Follow its choices
   on hyphenation, capitalisation of named objects, and whether an author's
   name attached to a result is possessive.

5. **Keep numbering consistent with the source.** Where the statement numbers
   conditions, cases, or equations, refer to them by those numbers rather than
   introducing a parallel scheme of your own.

6. **Use the alphabet of the statement.** Where Greek or script letters appear,
   reproduce them rather than transliterating, and keep sub- and superscripts
   in the positions the statement gives them.

7. **Leave the statement's abbreviations intact** wherever they recur, rather
   than expanding them on each use or introducing new contractions.
